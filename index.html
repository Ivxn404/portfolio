02HypothesisDoctoral intent

What a non-invasive measurement can recover depends on the head it is recorded from — not only the montage.

Doctoral proposal · Fall 2027 Anatomy-Conditioned Recoverability in Non-Invasive Neural Measurement

The proposal argues that identifiability of cortical mechanism from scalp measurement is conditional on individual head anatomy, not a fixed property of an electrode layout. The method is a simulation chain in which ground truth is known by construction, so recoverability can be measured rather than assumed.

It grew out of a failure. My ISRO work reconstructed EEG from ECG at high fidelity within cohort, then collapsed on a held-out subject set. Rather than retire the result, I took the question seriously: what is it about a person that decides whether a model transfers to them?

What this shows. Two cortical sources are projected onto a 16-electrode ring through a simplified single-layer dipole kernel, with the skull modelled as a circular spatial low-pass — the qualitatively right effect, since the skull smears high spatial frequencies. κ is the condition number of the two-column lead field: the ratio of its singular values, taken exactly from the 2×2 Gram matrix. Because κ is scale-invariant, only geometry and that low-pass can move it. Try this: leave the sources where they are and drag the skull from 4 mm to 14 mm. κ rises from about 2.8 to about 10 and the verdict flips — same sources, same electrodes, different head. That is the thesis in one gesture. Pull the sources apart and κ falls; push them together and it diverges, because two causes now produce one measurement. Illustrative model, not a solver.

Thread 01 · Identifiability

A forward-and-inverse simulation chain where the generating mechanism is specified, so what a montage can and cannot recover becomes a measured quantity conditioned on anatomy.

Which cortical mechanisms are recoverable from the scalp for this head, and which are structurally invisible?

Thread 02 · Transfer

The ISRO cross-modal result held within cohort and failed across subjects. The A*STAR attachment takes the next step: selective wet-to-dry transfer, with negative transfer built into the analysis rather than hidden by an average.

Can recordings from other people reduce calibration for a new one — and who is made worse off when they do?

Thread 03 · Neurophotonics

Finite-element modelling of near-infrared photon diffusion in cortical tissue, validated against Monte Carlo, plus whole-body NIR transport across heterogeneous anatomy — the physics side of the same question.

How far can diffuse optics be pushed toward reliable cortical readout outside a controlled lab?

01 A*STAR Agency for Science, Technology and Research Research Attachment — EEG Manifold Representations for Cognition Institute of Advanced Intelligence and Computing (IAIC) · Singapore · Supervisors: Dr. Phua Kok Soon, Koh Tze Hui 07 Dec 2026 — 16 Apr 2027

Final-semester bachelor's thesis attachment under VIT's Semester Abroad Programme: transferring selected representations from a wet-electrode EEG encoder to a dry-electrode encoder, so a low-dimensional frontal manifold can support headband-grade inference.

Central question — can wet EEG collected from other people reduce the calibration a new person needs on dry EEG?

Three sub-questions, each with its own reporting standard: whether it generalises to new people; whether it reduces labelled-trial requirements; and who it harms — individual gains and losses reported separately, since a higher mean can conceal negative transfer.

Baselines first, then selective transfer with controlled ablations as the main modelling contribution. Public wet and dry EEG recordings across 102 subjects.

An optional final block tests whether any benefit survives reducing dry input from eight channels to four predefined posterior ones.

09

Automated FE Pipeline for Flexible Neural Implant Design

From-scratch Q4 plane-strain solver, 56,364 DOF, plus a reduced-order beam-on-Winkler-foundation model, analysing micromotion-induced strain across materials, alloy compositions, section shapes, sizes and filler concentrations.

7,400 configurations solved, 2,161 feasible. Verified through six internal checks (V1–V6) and five cross-validation checks (X1–X5) — the verification chain is the credibility backbone. ANSYS cross-validation in progress.

Best candidate: polyimide with 30 vol% Ti-6Al-4V in a U-channel section. Section shape alone yields ~3.4× less cross-sectional area and mass than the solid rectangles commercial probes use.
