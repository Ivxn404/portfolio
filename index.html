Ⅱ — Research directionDoctoral intent

What a non-invasive measurement can recover depends on the head it is recorded from — not only the montage.

Doctoral proposal Anatomy-Conditioned Recoverability in Non-Invasive Neural Measurement

The proposal argues that identifiability of cortical mechanism from scalp measurement is conditional on individual head anatomy, not a fixed property of an electrode layout. The method is a simulation chain in which ground truth is known by construction, so recoverability can be measured rather than assumed.

It grew out of a failure. My ISRO work reconstructed EEG from ECG at high fidelity within cohort, then collapsed on a held-out subject set. Rather than retire the result, I took the question seriously: what is it about a person that decides whether a model transfers to them?

Thread 01

Identifiability from non-invasive measurement

Building a forward-and-inverse simulation chain where the generating mechanism is specified, so that what a montage can and cannot recover becomes an measurable quantity conditioned on anatomy.

Which cortical mechanisms are recoverable from the scalp for this head, and which are structurally invisible?
Thread 02

What transfers across people and hardware

The ISRO cross-modal result held within cohort and failed across subjects on a held-out database. The A*STAR attachment takes the next step: selective wet-to-dry EEG transfer, with negative transfer built into the analysis rather than hidden by an average.

Can recordings from other people reduce calibration for a new one — and who is made worse off when they do?
Thread 03

Forward modelling & neurophotonics

Finite-element modelling of near-infrared photon diffusion in cortical tissue, validated against Monte Carlo, plus whole-body NIR transport across heterogeneous anatomy — the physics side of the same identifiability question.

How far can diffuse optics be pushed toward reliable cortical readout outside a controlled lab?

01 A*STAR Agency for Science, Technology and Research Research Attachment — EEG Manifold Representations for Cognition Institute of Advanced Intelligence and Computing (IAIC) · Singapore · Supervisors: Dr. Phua Kok Soon, Koh Tze Hui From Dec 2026

Final-semester bachelor's thesis attachment: transferring selected representations from a wet-electrode EEG encoder to a dry-electrode encoder, so that a low-dimensional frontal manifold can support headband-grade inference.

Central question — can wet EEG collected from other people reduce the calibration a new person needs on dry EEG?

Three sub-questions, each with its own reporting standard: whether it generalises to new people, whether it reduces labelled-trial requirements, and who it harms — individual gains and losses reported separately, since a higher mean can conceal negative transfer.

Baselines first, then selective transfer with controlled ablations as the main modelling contribution; public wet and dry EEG recordings across 102 subjects.

02 ISRO Indian Space Research Organisation Corresponding Project Trainee Human Space Flight Centre (HSFC) · Biomedical Engineering Division · Bengaluru May — Jun 2026

Built an independent cross-modal EEG/ECG reconstruction framework in BMED using transformer architectures with 1D-CNN synthesis, on telemetry comprising 5,000+ biological waveform segments.

Designed a high-frequency filtering engine to harmonise multimodal inputs with processing bounds up to 45 Hz.

Reconstruction reached >99% signal tracking within cohort — a self-recognition result, not cross-subject generalisation. Held out against an independent database, transfer failed.

That negative holdout is the finding I carried forward: it localises the problem to inter-subject and inter-hardware variability, and directly motivates the A*STAR selective-transfer project.

Diagnostic transformer classification mapping compressed global token matrices to 10 anomaly classes; training-loop convergence maintained under high dataset variance.

FEM Forward Modelling of Near-Infrared Photon Diffusion in Cortical Tissue

Finite-element photon transport model rebuilt in Python at 800 nm — 1,236 nodes, 2,352 triangular elements — with a three-stage verification chain.

Continuous-wave diffusion solves with gray-matter-matched optical parameters; under 0.5% variance across mesh refinements.

Written up as a seven-page IEEE-format manuscript with 32 references. Not yet submitted.
