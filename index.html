Ⅰ — About

I build the instruments that listen to the body — and the models that understand what they hear.

I'm an Electronics & Communication Engineering student at VIT Vellore, specialising in Biomedical Engineering. My work sits on the boundary between embedded hardware and computational neuroscience: designing the sensing circuit, writing the real-time conditioning, and building the model that turns a raw waveform into something a clinician — or a flight surgeon — can act on.

At ISRO's Human Space Flight Centre I develop cross-modal deep learning frameworks for EEG and ECG telemetry, reconstructing and classifying physiological signals for astronaut fatigue and anomaly detection.

Earlier work at CSIR-CMERI covered analog sensor instrumentation for assistive robotics and CNN-based histopathology classification, and an industrial placement took me into vibration monitoring and PCB design for CNC spindle diagnostics.

My research interests centre on neuro-engineering, diffuse optical tomography, and edge-deployable biomedical AI — particularly systems that stay reliable outside the lab, in resource-limited or high-stakes environments.

Ⅱ — Research directionDoctoral intent

I want to build the instruments that read brain state — and the inference layer that makes them mean something.

My trajectory has been from biomedical engineer toward neuroscientist: I started at the circuit — stretch sensors, accelerometers, embedded FFT on an STM32 — and moved steadily up the stack toward the question of what a physiological signal actually tells you about the system producing it.

A direct PhD is the right vehicle because the problems I care about don't separate cleanly into hardware and analysis. A photon transport model is only as useful as the probe geometry it assumes; a seizure classifier is only as useful as the wearable it can run on. I want to work across that whole span.

Thread 01

Multimodal neural decoding

Cross-modal reconstruction between EEG and ECG, and Transformer-based classification of physiological anomaly — currently applied to astronaut fatigue telemetry and a Human Digital Twin programme at ISRO.

How much of one modality's information is latent in another — and what does shared structure reveal about underlying physiological state?
Thread 02

Non-invasive optical neuroimaging

Finite-element forward modelling of near-infrared photon diffusion in cortical tissue, validated against Monte Carlo, and whole-body NIR transport simulation across heterogeneous anatomy.

How far can diffuse optics be pushed toward reliable cortical readout outside a controlled lab environment?
Thread 03

Edge-deployable biomedical inference

Real-time embedded DSP, 1D-CNN seizure detection built for ambulatory monitoring, and wearable tremor sensing — models constrained by what a microcontroller on a person's body can actually sustain.

What survives the move from GPU to embedded target, and what does that constraint teach us about the signal?
